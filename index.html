Crypto trading bot
fees and execution time

Trading fees apply to each fill

Use your exchange's maker and taker rates. Limit orders that immediately cross the order book can incur taker fees. Enter each side's fee in the calculator.

Frequency affects total costs

Tighter grids and smaller rebalancing thresholds can increase turnover. Compare cumulative fees with the gross margin captured, plus any subscription price in the dashboard.

Orders fill when the market matches

A trigger starts an order request. API response time, exchange rate limits, liquidity and queue position determine acceptance and filling. A limit order can remain open until price reaches it.

Spot and futures have different costs

Spot strategies trade available balances. Perpetual futures add funding payments, margin requirements and liquidation thresholds to the strategy's cost and exposure.

Crypto trading bot security starts with access controls

A dedicated trading key and clear account limits make automated orders easier to control. Configure these settings before activating an exchange connection.

  • Trade-only permissions

    Enable only the market and account permissions your strategy uses. Keep API withdrawals disabled.

  • Dedicated account limits

    Allocate a separate budget or subaccount. Set position and order-size limits for each strategy.

  • Restricted API access

    Use an IP allowlist where supported. Rotate keys regularly and revoke connections you stop using.

  • Defined stop behavior

    Decide whether pausing cancels pending orders and whether an exit closes the current position.

Best Crypto Trading Bot FAQ

Practical answers about strategy selection, trading fees, exchange access and the numbers that matter.

What is the Best Crypto Trading Bot for my strategy?

Choose a grid bot to trade repeated price moves inside a range, a DCA bot to buy a fixed amount on a schedule, or a rebalancing bot to maintain portfolio weights. Compare the trigger, capital requirements and exit controls before choosing a configuration.

How does a crypto grid trading bot work?

A grid bot splits a price range into levels. It places buy orders at lower levels and sell orders at higher levels, then replaces filled orders according to the grid rules. A geometric grid uses the same percentage distance between adjacent levels. Trading fees reduce the margin on each completed pair of orders.

How much money do I need for a crypto trading bot?

The minimum budget depends on the exchange minimum order size and the number of funded orders. At $10 per order, 12 equally funded orders need at least $120 before fees and reserves. Check minimum quantity, price precision and the required base and quote balances for your selected pair.

Can I use a trading bot for Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana?

Use BTC, ETH or SOL trading pairs available on your connected exchange. Select a quote asset such as USDT or USDC, then set a separate budget and rules for each pair. Liquidity, order minimums and fee tiers differ by market.

What fees should I include in a trading bot strategy?

Include the maker or taker fee on both the buy and sell, the bid-ask spread, slippage and any bot subscription charge. A 0.10% buy fee and a 0.10% sell fee cost about 0.20% across a round trip relative to the initial order value. Funding payments also apply to perpetual futures positions.

Does a crypto trading bot need withdrawal access?

Use an exchange API key with read and trade permissions, and keep withdrawals disabled. Restrict the key to the required account, apply an IP allowlist when supported, and revoke unused keys from the exchange settings.

What happens when the price leaves the grid range?

A fixed grid has no additional levels outside its boundaries. Below the lower limit, the position becomes more concentrated in the base asset; above the upper limit, it becomes more concentrated in the quote asset. Set a stop condition and review whether to pause, close or adjust the range.

What is the difference between grid profit and total profit?

Grid profit measures completed buy and sell cycles after their trading fees. Total profit also includes the changing value of open positions and other costs. Review realized profit, unrealized profit and total equity together when evaluating a bot.
